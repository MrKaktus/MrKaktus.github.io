**5010 motor housing** *2025- (Ubuntu)* Assembling 5010 motor housing and motor shaft. ## Housing Parts: - 1x Motor shell (3d printed) - [5010_Motor_shell.stl](./../../projects/2025_Humanoid/data/stl/5010_Motor_shell.stl) - [5010_Motor_shell.3mf](./../../projects/2025_Humanoid/data/3mf/5010_Motor_shell.3mf) - 4x M3 Threaded inserts (for attaching to gearbox housing): - [M3 x 4 x 5mm Threaded Insert Knurled Brass Nuts Female Thread Knurled](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B0BTYF2MMD) - 4x M2 Threaded inserts (for mounting rotary encoder): - [M2 x 3 x 3.5mm Threaded Insert Knurled Brass Nuts Female Thread Knurled](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B8GN63S2) ![All the parts before assembly.](./Housing_1_800.jpg) M3 and M2 threaded inserts need to be melted into motor housing using hot inerting process. Housing itself is quite small, so M3 inserts barely fit on its edge. M2 inserts should be placed inside - they will be used to connect rotary encoder circuit board. WARNING Calibration There is no way to place this magnet perfectly along motor shaft axis. It will always end up being slightly off-axis. Its more of a question how much that will be. This will be solved during motor and rotary encoder calibration process (so that its taken into notice when measuring motor shaft rotation). ![Magnet after securing it with glue gun.](./Housing_6_800.jpg) ## Housing and motor Parts: - 4x M3 x 8mm screws (sourced from 5010 motor box) ![All the parts before assembly.](./Housing_7_800.jpg) ![5010 motor secured inside of housing with M3x8mm screws.](./Housing_8_800.jpg) ## Motor shaft Parts: - 1x Motor shaft stiffener (3d printed) - [5010_Motor_Shaft.stl](./../../projects/2025_Humanoid/data/stl/5010_Motor_Shaft.stl) - [5010_Motor_Shaft.3mf](./../../projects/2025_Humanoid/data/3mf/5010_Motor_Shaft.3mf) - 4x [M3 x 0.5 x 10mm screws](https://a.co/d/0gWb3teZ) - 1x [M4 x 12mm Female Threaded Hex Standoff Pillar](https://a.co/d/07ojR2v6) Tools: - 1x [4mm Metric M35 Cobalt Steel Twist Drill Bit](https://a.co/d/0bUsTGEk) Motor shaft assembly and its further connection to input shaft, in my opinion are the most crucial part of the whole robot design. Those shafts will carry all the force from motor to gearbox and further to the limbs. It will be exposed to great forces and under constantly changing load. Thats why I expect it to be the first thing to break and/or be impacted by assembly imperfections. ![All the parts before assembly.](./Housing_9_800.jpg) Motor shaft has 4mm diameter. It will be extended with M4x12mm hex standoff pillar inserted onto it. Because mentioned hex standoff is threaded, its ID is smaller than 4mm and needs to be slightly widened. To do that, I used 4mm cobalt drill. We want mentioned hex standoff to fit snugly onto motor shaft, to ensure they both withstand forces applied to them. Thats why its important to drill through that standoff carefull, just barely widening it. I let drill "fall through" the hex stand, without applying any excessive force to it, thats the only moment when I keep drilling machine on. Then I immediatelly turn it off, so that when I move it up again to pull out drill from the hex standoff, its off already and its not applying any force to that standoff. This way I still feel the resistance when I pull it out, and I know that the ID was only slightly increased, leaving it sill snug fit for motor shaft. !!! WARNING Calibration If you think at this point its all bonkers, extremely imprecise and gonzo development, you're completely right. Its equally silly like Robert Downey Jr. pretending to build Iron Man suit in the cave. This has nothing to do with word "precise". Yet its the way to make those parts connect in a prototype. While designing from scratch my next humanoid robot, I plan to rely on proper manufacturing approaches like CNC milling. This definitely needs higher standards of precision. Hopefully we can mitigate any off-axis errors here with calibration. ![Widening hex stand ID with cobalt dril.](./Housing_10_800.jpg) To make connection between motor shaft and hext standoff more durable, the two will be surrounded by 3d printed stiffener (called motor shaft too). We need to ensure that insering hext standoff into it will be tight as well. Thats where I hit precision issues with the print. More details you will find in: [Precision issues](./../2026_02_27_Precision_issues/2026_02_27_Precision_issues.html) When motor shaft stiffener is attached to motor body with M3x10mm screws, original motor shaft (metal one seen in previous photos) is now inside of the 3d printed part, where hex stand will go. M4x12mm hex standoff then needs to be inserted into motor shaft stiffener, and onto that metal shaft itself. If the hext standoff hole is not big enough, it won't go all the way in, but there is no way to easily tell if thats the case after the fact. I could use calipers to measure hole depth in metal shaft stiffener and then measure how much of the hex stanoff sticks out, but its much easier to mark it upfront. I simply insert hex stand into 3d printed motor shaft ahead of time, and mark how deep it goes with the marker. ![Marking how deep hext standoff should go in.](./Housing_11_800.jpg) If there is any misalignment, or hex standoff inner diameter is not wide enough yet, it won't go all the way in onto motor shaft, and instead of one line we will have two, making it obvious. ![Final assembly.](./Housing_12_800.jpg) As with other 3d printed parts,
