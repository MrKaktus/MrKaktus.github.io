**Gearmotor precision issues** *2025- (Ubuntu)* 3d printers are great for prototyping, allowing quick iteration times, but they fall short when sub-milimeter precision is needed. Thats exactly what happened in this project when it came to printing gearmotor parts. Motor shaft, input shaft and output shaft - those are the key parts that connect motor to reduction geartrain and transfer all the forces that end up moving the robot. Those are very small parts, that need to fit perfectly and withstand great forces. Printing those parts properly came out to be the biggest challenge on the mechanical side of this project, and it took me at least a month of tweaking and experimentation before I finally found satisfying solution (bear in mind its month in "on/off after hours" timescale). In future designs I plan to switch to CNC milled aluminum for crucial components like actuators and structural elements. ## Encountered issues ### Motor shaft and input shaft ![Zoom in on hex stand being loose in M6C12 motor shaft stiffener.](./Precision_1_800.jpg) Both motor and input shaft prints came out to have internal diameter too big by about ~0.5mm, causing inserted into them hex stands to be loose. ### Output shaft front I've spent most of my time trying to get M6C12 output shaft front to print within acceptable precision limits. The biggest issue was that 6 x 10 × 3mm ball bearings did not fit on output shaft front six columns. Some bearings themselves came out to have internal diameter 0.05mm narrower than specified (5.95mm instead of 6mm). At the same time mentioned columns had their outer diameter varying in size by 0.15mm. Both errors compounded preventing bearings insertion. As WA to this issue, I've decided to manually sand off mentioned pillars (six on each output shaft front part) to reduce their diameter to ~5.95mm. To do this I bought sand paper with P220 grit/grain, and used strips of it to sand those pillars off with my fingers. It was tedious and time consuming work that ended up with blisters, as there were 10 such parts to sand off. Thakfully by the time I've started assembling 5010 geartrains, I've found solution to mentioned precision issues (which was combination of all corrections described below). This is one of those moments, when you wish you were smarter and spent more time on search for proper solution instead of wasting it on WAs. ### Gears Gears got impacted by heat disperion related shrinking. Distortions were so bad that when assembled, those gears wouldn't fit into gearbox housing, or if they did, they wouldn't be able to rotate in it. ![Warped gears on the left vs correct one on the right.](./Precision_2_800.jpg) ## Corrections ### Belts tension While it is one of complete basics of 3d printing, and its not directly related to gearmotor precision issues, its very important to remember about regular testing of belts tension, and its adjustment. It was first time, when I was 3d printing anything in such volume. Spools of fillament would go one after another, and I forgot to tune belts tension after each one was finished (which I do now). Omission to do that lead to below failure: ![Too loose Y-axis belt.](./Precision_3_800.jpg) ### Overextruding To verify if 3d printer is not overextruding, I've printed calibration cube. With extrusion width set to 0.45mm and 1 perimiter, cube wall should be exactly 0.45mm thick. ![Calibration cube with walls too thick by 0.03mm](./Precision_4_800.jpg) As you can see I've got cube walls 0.03mm thicker than expected. This means my printer was indeed slightly overextruding. To fix this I used equation: - NewFlow = 0.45 / MeasuredThickness - NewFlow = 0.45 / 0.48 - NewFlow = 0.9375 - New flow is 94% In PrusaSlicer options: - Filaments > Filament > Filament > Extrusion Multiplier: changed value from 1 to 0.94 ### XY Compensation Fixing overextrusion wasn't enough. I needed to apply XY compensation on final model, to further reduce the error in resulting prints. PrusaSlicer documentation for XY compensation is really confusing here. It implies that provided compensation value will scale size of whole model by adding that value, but thats not the case. Before I figured it out I wasted plenty of time on experimenting with this value while trying to solve output shaft precision issues. ![Multiple failed attempts of scaling output shaft.](./Precision_5_800.jpg) What XY compensation really means is as follows: - Negative value INCREASES hole sizes, but DECREASES overall size. Print is sunken / thinned. - Positive value DECREASES hole size, but INCREASES overall size. Print is swollen. Setting this value to +0.05mm finally solved motor and input shaft issues, making them tight when inserting hex standoff into them. ### Shrinking solution Initially I was printing parts using Hatchbox PLA pure white fillament adviced in original documentation. Things were going ok with first spool, but then US tariffs went in effect. Hatchbox moved its fillament production into USA. Since then, fillament that was supposed to be pure white was more of pistachio in color, and its quality went down. I wasn't able to make it properly stick to the print bed, and even if I did, it would suffer from mentioned heat disperion related shrinking. I've switched to Creality PLA white, which has beautiful white color and good printing properties. For printing itself I've decided to print each part separately (instead of filling whole print plate with multiple parts), applying brim of 5mm and adding support structures everywhere. I've got to conclusion that its better to be conservative, than wasting time on re-printing warped parts.
